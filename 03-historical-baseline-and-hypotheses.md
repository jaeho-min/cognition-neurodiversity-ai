## Why the childhood record matters 
 
The childhood record gives me a historical reference point that existed long before I developed this interpretation of my AI use. 
 
Importantly, the order was: 
 
- I first noticed changes in how I approached problems. 
- I then searched for old psychological records. 
- Only after that did I discover the childhood ADHD diagnosis. 
 
The ADHD history therefore did not originally provide the framework through which I interpreted the perceived change. 
 
That does not prove that the change is objective. 
 
It does not prove that AI caused it. 
 
But it gives me something older and independent to compare against. 
 
 
## What I want to test 
 
The question is not whether an IQ of 112, 118, 103 or an estimated potential of 128+ defines who I am. 
 
The question is what those historical measurements look like when compared with my current cognitive profile and with the strategies developed during long-term AI use. 
 
Possible explanations include: 
 
- measurable cognitive change 
- better access to abilities that were previously expressed inconsistently 
- improved executive regulation 
- learned cognitive strategies 
- internalization of structures repeatedly practiced with AI 
- performance gains that exist only while AI is available 
- ordinary adult development 
- accumulated knowledge and experience 
- or some combination of these
